### 3. I have received and understood explanations regarding the intent and usefulness of this repository.

Verified: yes

Explain:

I reviewed from the repository without a live session, so this is the intent as the README and PROMPT.md state it. The app is for someone who measures ferromagnetic resonance of a magnetic insulator film with field sweeps and a lock in amplifier instead of a VNA. You give it a folder of sweeps, one CSV per frequency, and it fits every resonance line, then fits frequency against field (Kittel) and linewidth against frequency to get the g factor, the effective perpendicular field Beff,⊥ and the Gilbert damping α with 1σ errors. It works for in plane and out of plane geometries. It is useful because it replaces fitting 49 lines by hand and keeps every sweep inspectable, with linked exclusions so a bad trace leaves both fits at once. On the bundled data the check in reports g ≈ 1.996, Beff,⊥ ≈ 0.949 T and α ≈ 0.0049.

The damping failure is a tolerance of 1e-10 when the constant itself is only known to about 3e-10, so the code is fine there. The bound failure is a real finding. The in plane model has an exact mirror solution, so a film with Beff,⊥ of minus 0.1 T comes back as Beff,⊥ of plus 0.1 T and B4 of minus 0.1 T with R² of 1.0 and no warning. On the demo data I would trust g and Beff,⊥ (Kittel R² 0.996, and the bounds rule the mirror out there) but not α yet. The linewidth fit behind α has R² 0.096 and a negative intercept of minus 14.5 ± 17 Oe, three linewidths sit far off the line, and the page itself warns that 44 of 49 sweeps have modulation above ΔHpp/3, so the measured linewidths are probably broadened. The new warnings are doing their job, but the α card is still the highlighted one. On an unknown film I would not trust Beff,⊥ and B4 until the mirror case is flagged. The broadband golden values are the app's own earlier output with ±20 % on α, so they are a tripwire and not evidence, which the check in says too.
