A SHORT INTRODUCTION

What this app measures, and why it matters

What is ferromagnetic resonance?

The magnetization of a magnetic film behaves like a spinning top: pushed away from the applied field, it precesses around it. Microwaves at the precession frequency are absorbed. Sweeping the field at a fixed microwave frequency and recording where the absorption happens, and how wide the line is, is ferromagnetic resonance (FMR).

What comes out

  • g-factor: how fast the magnetization precesses per tesla. Close to 2 for a spin-only magnet; a shift points to orbital contributions in the material.
  • Effective perpendicular field Beff,⊥: how strongly the film prefers its magnetization to lie in the plane (positive) or point out of it (negative). It tells you whether growth produced the anisotropy you wanted.
  • Four-fold field B4: a small in-plane term from the crystal symmetry along the measured axis.
  • Gilbert damping α: how quickly the precession dies out. It is the number most devices care about.

So what?

Magnetic insulator films are candidates for spin-wave (magnonic) and spintronic devices, which carry information with spin rather than charge. Low damping means spin waves travel further and magnetization switches with less current, so α decides whether a film is useful. Measuring it by hand means fitting dozens of resonance lines one by one; this app fits all of them, shows each fit, and tells you when a result should not be trusted yet.

How to use it

  1. Pick the geometry (field in the film plane, or normal to it) and upload a folder with one CSV sweep per frequency, or explore the bundled demo data.
  2. Read the four results and their 1σ errors, and read the yellow warnings.
  3. Click points that look wrong, inspect their raw sweeps, and exclude them; both fits update.
